# Draft 03 edit learnings

*Editorial history only. Not website content. Compare `topic-18-ai-draft.md` with `topic-18-human-edit.md`.*

Topic 18 was the third and final Phase 0 voice test: thought leadership. Can Brandible take a clear position without becoming motivational, preachy, overly clever, or generic. The AI draft already had the right argument, the right structure, and most of the strongest lines. The human pass was not a voice rewrite. It was editorial: keep this article from becoming a second Topic 19.

Draft 01 already taught restraint, no fake “most,” no clever lines, no disguised listicles, no promotional CTA pivot, no em dashes. Draft 02 already taught honesty in numbers, labeled Brandible claims, unnamed-client decoration, and self-qualifying CTAs. Do not re-learn those here. This file only records what Topic 18 added, what it validated, and what was local to this topic.

## What changed

**Article ownership.** The AI draft reused Topic 19’s website symptoms and exercises: slogan and stock photo, homeowners vs businesses / one town vs region, unfinished site, tap-to-call, the essay form, search-your-service-and-town, send the homepage, try to hire yourself on a phone. The human edit kept the same journey (find, understand, trust, buy) and wrote it as this article’s problem: a stranger cannot see or hire the quality that already exists. That was the main edit. It is an editorial and content-planning problem, not a voice problem.

**Diagnostic belongs to the thesis.** AI used Topic 19’s homepage/UX tests. Human replaced them with a question only this article needs: can a stranger see evidence of the quality regular customers already know about. The homepage test remains correct for Topic 19. It was wrong here because this article is not about a conversion leak on a page people already reached.

**First-party story, shortened.** The Doorstep Detailer stayed. “A local detailing business, before the agency existed” did not. What remained is the lesson: doing the work well wasn’t the hard part. The origin sentence supports the argument. The company-history extra did not.

**“Exclusive / exist.”** Cut the pull-quote pair. Replaced with the plain point: they don’t wait around; they hire someone they can find.

**Word of mouth.** AI: “it’s slow.” Human: referrals only help when the potential customer is already connected to someone who knows the business. Same territory. Honest mechanism instead of a convenient adjective.

**Examples and storefront.** Contractor, consultant, and restaurant stayed, tightened. “A clearer website won’t fix inconsistent work” became “better marketing won’t,” so the close does not collapse Topic 18 into a website article. The storefront line stayed.

**CTA.** “A new site won’t create it” became “Brandible can’t create it.” Same self-disqualification. More direct, and not website-only.

The open, the thesis sentence, “They see what’s in front of them this week,” “They were willing. You made them work,” and the short close were already right. They were not rewritten.

## Why it changed

Topic 18 and Topic 19 sit next to each other. Both involve strangers, websites, proof, and a next step. The AI draft treated that overlap as permission to reuse the conversion article’s pictures. The result sounded like Brandible and still failed the assignment: two articles that could swap titles.

A diagnostic is not a Brandible ritual. It is a test of the claim this piece is making. “Send the homepage to someone who doesn’t know the business” tests whether a visit converts. “Can a stranger see the quality your regulars already know about” tests whether excellent work is visible outside the existing circle. Only the second one belongs here.

Doorstep belongs in this article because the insight is the company’s origin sentence. It does not belong as a second About-page paragraph. Named first-party experience that teaches the point stays. Origin-story padding goes.

“Word of mouth is slow” is the same class of problem as “most people will see it on mobile.” It is not a fake census, but it is still an unsupported universal about a mechanism. The precise limit was available. Use that.

## Permanent Voice Guide lessons (from this comparison)

1. Adjacent articles must earn their own reason to exist. Do not recycle the same examples, diagnostics, symptoms, or structure across related topics. Define what question each article owns and keep the writing centered on that question.
2. Use first-party experience when it explains how Brandible learned something, not simply to mention Brandible.

These are now in the Voice Guide (point of view, sections 6 and 13, ban list, checklist items 5, 8, and 10).

The diagnostic principle is part of lesson 1, not a separate rule. Always give the reader a test. The test must belong to this argument. Do not turn “can a stranger see the quality” into the required diagnostic for every post. That test is Topic 18’s.

## Existing rules this draft validated

- No clever pull-quote lines. “They don’t think you’re exclusive. They think you might not exist” is the same family as “the whole game.”
- No unsupported universals. “Word of mouth is slow” is a slogan-adjective. Describe the actual limitation instead. This does not need a new rule beyond Draft 01’s honesty rule and the editorial standard’s “describe the mechanism.”
- Short close. Do not restate the whole argument. The AI close was already short. The human pass left it short.
- Self-qualifying CTA, including when Brandible is not the fit. Thought leadership needed it too: if the work is not there yet, Brandible cannot create that quality. That reinforces Draft 02. It is not a new numbered rule. Section 9 of the Voice Guide now says self-qualify whenever the CTA names Brandible, not only on commercial pieces.
- Named work vs decoration. Doorstep stayed because it is named and it teaches. Unnamed client decoration was already banned.
- No em dashes, contractions, say it once, no disguised listicle, no motivational language. The AI draft already passed these. The human edit did not have to teach them again.

## Article-specific (do not turn into general rules)

- The contractor, consultant, and restaurant examples. They fit this thesis. Future articles should pick scenes that fit *their* question.
- The exact Doorstep wording. The rule is first-party that teaches. This sentence belongs to this post.
- “They see what’s in front of them this week.” “They were willing. You made them work.” Keep them here. Do not require them elsewhere.
- “Don’t buy a better storefront for a product you wouldn’t stand behind.” One-off image for this argument.
- The quality-evidence diagnostic as a required exercise. The *principle* (the test matches the thesis) is reusable. This exact question is Topic 18’s.
- Using “make it easy to choose you” once in the close. That is how this piece earned the positioning line. It is not a quota.
- “If the work isn’t there yet, Brandible can’t create it.” The rule is self-qualify. The wording belongs to this article.

## Voice vs editorial

The remaining edits were not “this doesn’t sound like Brandible.” The AI draft already sounded like Brandible: confident position, customer scenes, short close, self-qualifying CTA, no em dashes, no fake “most.” What failed was article planning. Two adjacent thought-leadership pieces need two jobs, two tests, and two takeaways. That is the Phase 0 finding.

The two drafts remain side by side. Do not publish this file.

## Phase 0 conclusion

**Did the three tests demonstrate a stable Brandible voice?** Yes, across the three types Phase 0 asked for. Draft 01 (problem-aware / Topic 19), Draft 02 (commercial-intent / Topic 01), and Draft 03 (thought leadership / Topic 18) all used the same spine: name the belief, take a position, show what a customer actually does, give a test, close short, CTA that stays inside the problem. By Draft 03 the AI output already sounded like the site talking at article length. It did not become motivational, preachy, or generic. The thought-leadership test passed on voice.

**Did human editing decrease or become more specialized?** It became more specialized, and the amount of voice rewriting dropped. Draft 01 was a voice pass: clever lines, restated thesis, disguised listicle, em dashes, fake “most,” promotional CTA. Draft 02 kept the argument and corrected honesty (whose prices, what “usually” means, unnamed decoration, self-qualify). Draft 03 kept the argument and corrected article ownership, first-party length, and one mechanism claim. That is the pattern you want before Phase 1: less “make it sound like us,” more “keep this piece honest and distinct.”

**Are the remaining issues voice issues?** No. They are normal editorial judgment: sourcing and precision, which examples belong, how much first-party story to use, and especially whether this article is doing a job another article already does. The Voice Guide already covers how Brandible sounds. Draft 03 showed that sounding right is not the same as earning a reason to exist next to a sibling post.

**Is the Voice Guide mature enough to move into Phase 1?** Yes, as a drafting standard, not as a substitute for human edit. It now covers the three article types Phase 0 tested, plus the two lessons Draft 03 added (adjacent articles, first-party that teaches). More Phase 0 voice tests would mostly rediscover the same rules. Phase 1 should still compare AI against a human pass, especially on article differentiation, labeled Brandible facts, and diagnostics that belong to the thesis. It should not keep extracting voice from scratch.

Do not start CMS files, scripts, or publishing logic from this conclusion. The next step is a human decision about what Phase 1 is, using this guide.
