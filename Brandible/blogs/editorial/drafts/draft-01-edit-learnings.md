# Draft 01 edit learnings

*Editorial history only. Not website content. Compare `topic-19-ai-draft.md` with `topic-19-human-edit.md`.*

Topic 19 was the first Phase 0 test: AI draft, then a human-editor pass that kept the argument and cut about 17%. These are the differences that should change how the next draft is written.

## What stayed (do not unlearn)

The opening move was right: usual fix (buy more attention), then the real problem (the site wastes the visit).

The strongest lines survived because they were concrete:

- “You paid for the visit. The site talked them out of taking the next step.”
- “They were ready to reach out and you asked them to write an essay.”
- The homepage test: send the page to someone who doesn’t know the business.

The diagnostic section was already the best part. It was not heavily cut. Future drafts should treat a practical split like that as load-bearing, not optional.

## Major differences

**Title.** AI used an unsourced universal: “Most Small Businesses Don’t Have a Traffic Problem.” Human edit kept the argument and narrowed the scope: “You Might Not Have a Traffic Problem. You Might Have a Website Problem.” Confident, not a fake census.

**Length and repetition.** The AI draft restated the thesis at the open, after the landing section, after the leak, and three more times in the close. The human edit said it once up top and let the rest prove it. The close went from five paragraphs to two: takeaway, then CTA.

**Clever phrasing.** Cut: “Those seconds are the whole game,” “throwing money at a wall,” “that conclusion is comforting,” “Think about how a stranger actually meets your business online.” Kept: short, blunt, useful. Brandible should explain, not write pull quotes.

**Unsupported claims.** Cut “which is how most people will see it” (implied mobile statistic). The same idea is now “Then they open it on their phone” plus the actual failures (menu, type size, no tap-to-call).

**Disguised listicle.** The AI leak section was seven labeled problems in seven paragraphs (messaging, trust, mobile, speed, nav, CTA, journey). The human edit followed the visit: they land and can’t tell who it’s for, they try the phone, they hunt, they try to reach out. Related issues were merged instead of itemized.

**Customer scene vs marketing terms.** “Trust is thin” became the pictures: no real photos, no names, no reviews, old copyright. “Poor mobile UX” was already better as “I’ll try the next one.” Keep going in that direction. Show what they see, think, or do.

**CTA.** AI switched into promo: “That’s the work we do on the web design side, not a prettier brochure. See how we build sites that convert.” Human edit stayed in the article’s problem: if traffic is reaching the site and inquiries still aren’t happening, that is what Brandible’s web design and development service is built to solve.

**Em dashes.** The AI draft used them in the intro, diagnostic, and CTA. The human edit used commas, periods, and parentheses only. This is now a permanent hard rule.

## What the Voice Guide should do next time

1. Argue with confidence. Do not invent “most.”
2. Prefer the plain sentence over the memorable one.
3. Say a good point once.
4. Walk with the customer. Do not file issues as a list.
5. Show the scene. Do not stop at the label.
6. Give at least one test the reader can run this week.
7. Short close. One takeaway. CTA as the next sentence of the advice.
8. No em dashes.

The two drafts remain side by side so later edits can be checked against this one. Do not publish this file.
