# Brandible Editorial Standard

*Phase 0. Companion to the Voice Guide. Last updated: August 31, 2026.*

This standard covers what a Brandible article *is*, how it is structured, what it must not invent, and how it should be filed in the existing CMS. It does not describe automation.

---

## Purpose of the blog

The blog exists to help local business owners make better decisions about their website, brand, search, content, and systems — and to make Brandible the obvious partner when they are ready for help.

It is not a newsroom. It is not a glossary. It is not a place to republish “what is SEO” again.

Every post should do at least two of these:

- Answer a question a buyer is already asking (cost, timeline, “why isn’t this working,” ads vs SEO)
- Correct a belief that costs them money
- Give them a way to judge their current site, listing, or marketing
- Point naturally to a Brandible service when the work is bigger than a DIY fix

## What we learned from the four existing posts

Use these posts to copy **voice and rhythm**. Do not copy **topic and altitude**.

| Post | What to keep | What not to repeat |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Welcome (Dec 2025) | Clear promise of practical writing; invitation to email | Meta “what this blog is” content. One welcome is enough. |
| Why your website matters (Dec 2025) | Opening claim, “three things on the page,” conversion as small details | Another generic “websites are important” essay |
| What is SEO (Jan 2026) | Plain-English definition, local SEO emphasis, ads-vs-SEO contrast | Another SEO 101. Go deeper: GBP, “near me,” why *this* site isn’t ranking |
| Social media for small businesses (Mar 2026) | Front-door metaphor, posting ≠ brand, anti-viral stance, service CTA | Another “social is the new storefront” piece |

Altitude gap: the blogs stay high-level. The website, FAQs, and About page are more useful (prices, timelines, process, Doorstep Detailer, month-to-month). **New posts should write at the FAQ/site level of usefulness, in the blog’s voice.**

## Article types

### Commercial / problem-aware (majority)

The reader has a problem or is comparing options. They are close to hiring someone.

- How much a website costs
- Why they don’t show up on Google
- Rebuild vs redesign
- Ads vs SEO
- Practical AI for missed calls

These should include enough specifics that a serious buyer can self-qualify.

### Educational / strategic

The reader needs a mental model they can use this week.

- What a homepage must communicate
- How local SEO differs from “SEO”
- How website, brand, and marketing fit together

Still take a position. Education is not neutrality.

### Thought leadership / opinion

Brandible says something it actually believes.

- Great work isn’t enough if people can’t find you
- Traffic problem vs website problem
- Operator-founded vs theory-led agencies

Opinion still needs examples. Hot takes without proof read as AI swagger.

## Default structure

Not every post must use every block. Most should follow this spine:

1. **Open** — claim or situation in 2–4 sentences
2. **What’s going on** — the mistake or pressure the reader is in
3. **The Brandible view** — the argument
4. **What to look at / do** — practical checks, examples, or a simple sequence
5. **What this is not** — kill the popular wrong answer (viral, one-page-does-everything, more posts, secret SEO tricks)
6. **Bottom line**
7. **One CTA** — relevant service or a free consult

Length:

- Aim **900–1,400 words** for commercial posts
- **700–1,000** for focused educational posts
- **600–900** for opinion pieces that stay tight

Shorter than ~600 usually means the piece is another thin explainer. Longer than ~1,600 usually means overexplaining. Cut.

The existing posts run roughly 550–800 words. That is a floor for voice, not a ceiling for usefulness.

## Headings

- H2s are sentences or clear jobs, not “Introduction / Benefits / Conclusion”
- H3s only when a section has distinct jobs (e.g. three homepage jobs)
- Do not number “7 Tips” as the whole outline
- Title: human, specific, no `| Brandible` suffix (the site already appends the brand). No keyword stuffing.

Good titles sound like the service-page headlines: “Custom sites that convert.” “Practical AI that actually helps your business run.”

---

## Evidence and honesty

- Do not invent statistics, quotes, rankings, or client results
- If you cite a number, it must be a Brandible-owned fact already on the site, or a named public source
- Brandible-owned facts you *may* use because they are already published:
  - Website projects typically 2–4 weeks
  - Web design typical investment $2,000–$10,000+
  - Digital marketing $750–$3,000+/month
  - Branding $1,500–$5,000+
  - Media management $400–$1,500+/month
  - AI & automation setup $750–$3,500+
  - Ads: spend billed to the platform; management often $600 setup + 15% of spend (min ~$400–$500/month spend)
  - Month-to-month agreements, 30 days’ notice
  - The Doorstep Detailer: built from scratch, #1 relevant local result in Hunterdon County, 250+ customers, five-star feedback
  - PEAC Learning: paid social/search generating tracked franchise leads
- If you don’t have a source, describe the mechanism instead of faking a study (“people decide in seconds” is experiential; “73% of consumers…” is not allowed unsourced)
- Never write “studies show” without naming the study

## Examples

Prefer:

- A local service business that is excellent in person and invisible on Google
- A site that lists every service on one scrolling page
- An Instagram that posts randomly with no clear offer
- A Google Business Profile with no photos, wrong hours, or ignored reviews
- After-hours missed calls
- Ads running with no conversion tracking

You can use Brandible work as illustration when it is true. One example per article is usually enough. The post is not a case-study dump unless the topic *is* a case study.

## Internal links

When it helps the reader, link to:

- The relevant service page (`/services/web-design/`, `/services/digital-marketing/`, `/services/branding/`, `/services/media-management/`, `/services/ai/`)
- `/contact/#book-call` for a consult
- An existing post only if it is actually related (do not force-link the welcome post)
- `/portfolio/` when “see the work” is the honest next step
- `/faqs/` when the article answers a question already in the FAQ — go deeper than the FAQ, then point to it

Category already maps to related services in the blog builder. Still put 1–3 contextual links in the body so the argument leads somewhere.

---

## CTAs

Rules:

- **One primary CTA** at the end. A second in-body link is fine if it is genuinely useful.
- Match the topic to **one** service. A post about missed calls goes to AI, not “all our services.”
- Tone: invitation, not urgency theater.
- Say what they get: a clear quote, a look at the site, a marketing call — not “get started on your journey.”

On-brand CTA shapes:

- “If the answer isn’t a confident yes, there’s a fix. [See how we build sites that convert →](/services/web-design/)”
- “If you want this handled without living in Ads Manager, [book a free marketing call](/contact/#book-call).”
- “We set up the practical kind — chat, follow-ups, after-hours. [See AI & Automation →](/services/ai/)”

Off-brand:

- “Contact Brandible Marketing Group today for all your digital needs!”
- “Limited spots this month.”
- CTA to the homepage with no reason.

## What not to cover (for now)

- Repeat SEO 101, “why you need a website,” “why social media matters,” or a second welcome post
- National enterprise marketing, VC/SaaS playbooks, or “for businesses of all sizes”
- Tool roundups (“best 15 AI tools in 2026”)
- Gossip about Google algorithm rumors unless Brandible has a practical local take
- Personal drama, politics, or hot industry dunks that don’t help the reader choose or improve

## CMS and file rules

Posts live in `Brandible/blogs/posts/`. The CMS collection is defined in `Brandible/admin/config.yml`.

**Filename is the URL.** Pattern: `YYYY-MM-DD-short-slug.md`. The builder strips the date and uses the rest as `/blogs/{slug}/`. Frontmatter `slug` is optional CMS metadata and **does not control the live URL**. Never put a leading slash in `slug`.

Required / expected fields:

| Field | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| `draft` | `true` until a human publishes |
| `title` | Human title. No `\| Brandible` suffix |
| `date` | ISO datetime |
| `author` | `Brandible Team` |
| `body` | Markdown. Short paragraphs. |
| `slug` | Optional. Lowercase hyphens, no leading `/`. Keep in sync with the filename slug. |
| `meta_title` | Optional but preferred. May be shorter than `title`. |
| `meta_description` | Optional but preferred. One or two sentences, no bait. |
| `excerpt` | Listing card copy. Useful, not teaser-y. |
| `featured_image` | `/assets/blog-images/...` when an image exists |
| `featured_image_alt` | Describe the image. Don’t keyword-stuff. |
| `og_image` | Only if share image should differ from featured |
| `category` | One of: Marketing, Web Design, SEO, Social Media, Business Tips, Case Studies |
| `tags` | Optional list. Use when they help related-service matching |

Images belong in `Brandible/assets/blog-images/`. Public path: `/assets/blog-images/filename.webp`.

Do not commit a generated post as published (`draft: false`) without human review. Phase 0 does not generate files at all until a topic is chosen.

## Author attribution

Use **Brandible Team** unless a named author is explicitly requested. Do not invent a guest expert. Do not byline “AI.”

Thought-leadership pieces may later be bylined to Mike when the piece is clearly the founder’s argument. That is a human decision, not a default.

---

## Quality bar before it is “a Brandible draft”

A draft is ready for human editing when:

1. Voice Guide checklist is 10/10, or the remaining fails are small and marked
2. Structure matches this standard (argument, not recap)
3. No unsourced numbers
4. One honest CTA
5. Frontmatter would be valid if saved into the CMS
6. It does not cannibalize an existing post’s topic

Phase 0 stops at the Voice Guide, this standard, the checklist, and the topic queue. No draft until a topic is selected on purpose.
