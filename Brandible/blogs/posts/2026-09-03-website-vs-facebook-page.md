The question comes up more than you'd think: "I've got a decent following, people comment on every post, I get inquiries through Messenger. Do I really need to pay for a website?"

It's a fair question. And the honest answer is: your Facebook Page is doing real work. But it's not doing the same work as a website.

## A Facebook Page and a Website Are Not the Same Tool

A Facebook Page lets you create a presence for your business on Facebook, reach your customers, share information, and run ads. [Source](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/477457986066066) That's genuinely useful. For some businesses, it's where a lot of early attention comes from.

But there's a difference between attention and trust. Between someone seeing your post and someone deciding to hire you.

When a customer gets close to making a decision, they start looking for signals that tell them you're the real thing. A website is where those signals live: your own domain, your own layout, your own photos, your own words, no algorithm deciding what they see next.

A Facebook Page can't do that the same way. It's rented space, on someone else's platform, with someone else's rules.

## What Happens When Someone's Ready to Hire You

Here's the pattern worth noticing. Someone sees your post, or a friend tags them, or they find your Page through a search. They like what they see. Now they want to know more.

They check your profile details: hours, address, a phone number, a website link. If that link goes nowhere, or there is no link, that's the first signal that something might be missing.

They might send a Messenger inquiry. That's good. But if they're comparing you to two other businesses, and both of those have a clear site with photos of their work, a service list, and a way to book online, your Messenger thread is competing with a better-prepared pitch.

A website isn't about looking fancy. It's about making it easy to choose you.

## The Platform Controls More Than You Think

You don't own the Page the way you own a website.

Your reach can drop. A feature you rely on can shift. Your Page can get reported, restricted, or buried by an algorithm update you had nothing to do with.

Your website doesn't work that way. You own it. It doesn't have a newsfeed competing with it. When someone looks you up, a website can show your copy, your offer, and your next step, on your terms.

## Where Facebook Stops and the Website Starts

Websites are built for conversion. They answer the deeper questions: What exactly do you offer? What does it cost? What do past customers say? How do I get started? Can I trust you with my house, my car, my event, my business?

Those two jobs belong together, not in competition.

A business that only has a Facebook Page is asking customers to make a hiring decision with less information than they'd want. A business that only has a website and no social presence is missing the discovery layer. You're usually better off with both: a Page that gets you found, and a site that helps someone choose you.

## Meta Business Suite Handles the Facebook Side Well

If you're running a Page seriously, Meta Business Suite lets you manage your Facebook and Instagram accounts in one place, view performance trends, respond to messages, and create ads. [Source](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/205614130852988?id=765488040896522) It's a real tool for staying consistent across platforms.

What it doesn't do is give you a place that's fully yours. No custom domain. No page structure you control. No ability to build the kind of trust that a well-built site creates when someone is close to booking.

Use the tools Meta gives you. And build a site that catches the people who want to go further.

## The Practical Test

Go find your own business the way a new customer would, not someone who already follows you, but someone who heard your name for the first time.

Google the service you offer plus your town. Does your business come up? If it does, where does the link go? If it doesn't come up at all, your Facebook Page is working harder than it should to carry everything.

Then open your Facebook Page on your phone as if you've never seen it. Is it clear what you do, who you help, and what the next step is? Now ask yourself: if someone wanted to hire you today, what would they click, where would they go, and how long before they either took a step or gave up?

That gap, between someone interested and someone booked, is what a website is built to close.

## A Good Facebook Page Is Worth Keeping

None of this is an argument to abandon Facebook. If your Page is active, well-reviewed, and bringing in inquiries, that's real. Keep going.

The point is that a strong Facebook Page and a working website do different jobs. One gets you found and recognized. The other gets you chosen. You need both sides of that, not one substituting for the other.
