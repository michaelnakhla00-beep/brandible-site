For a lot of local service businesses, the website gets all the attention. Time, money, the new photos. Meanwhile, the Google Business Profile sits there with wrong hours, a missing category, and three photos from 2021.

That profile is what people in your area see first. Before the website. Before the reviews page. Right there in Maps and Search, next to your name and phone number. Google surfaces local results based on relevance, distance, and prominence, and [a complete, accurate profile is one of the signals Google says helps businesses show up](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en). If the profile is incomplete or inaccurate, the visit to your site may never happen.

## What the Profile Actually Does

When someone searches for what you offer near them, Google surfaces local results based on a few signals: how relevant your profile is to the search, how close you are, and how prominent you appear based on reviews, activity, and the completeness of your listing. [Google explains this directly](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en): businesses with complete and accurate information are more likely to show up.

That means the profile isn't decoration. It's part of how you get found at all.

And once someone finds it, [Google's own performance data](https://support.google.com/business/answer/9918094?hl=en) tracks what they do next: views, clicks, calls, direction requests. Those actions tell you whether the profile is doing its job or just existing.

## What an Incomplete Profile Looks Like to a Stranger

Consider this from a customer's perspective. They search "electrician near me" on a Saturday morning. Three results show up in the map pack. One has 47 reviews and a 4.9 rating, a full list of services, recent photos of the team and finished work, and hours that confirm they're open right now. Another has 6 reviews, no photos, and hours that say "might not be accurate."

They call the first one.

That's not about the website. That's the profile.

The gap between those two listings often comes down to a few hours of setup that many businesses skip.

## The Parts That Actually Move the Needle

**Business category.** This is one of the most important fields and one of the most underused. Your primary category tells Google what kind of business you are, which determines when you show up. "General contractor" and "bathroom remodeler" are different categories. Choose the one that matches how people are searching for you, not the one that sounds most impressive.

**Hours.** Keep them accurate, including holidays. A profile that says you're open when you're not erodes trust fast. If your hours change seasonally, update them.

**Photos.** Profiles with photos give people something to judge before they call. Real photos of your work, your vehicle, your team, or your space perform better than stock images. [Google notes that higher-quality images can influence profile quality and may affect how profiles rank](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/7527305?hl=en).

**Services.** Google lets you list the specific services you offer. Use them. "Pressure washing," "gutter cleaning," and "deck staining" are all searchable. If you only list "exterior cleaning," you may miss the search that would have found you.

**Reviews.** You can't fake them and you can't buy them. But you can ask for them, and you can respond to the ones you have. A business with 40 reviews that responds to every one looks different from a business with 40 reviews and radio silence. Responding matters.

**Q&A.** Many businesses leave this blank. That's a missed opportunity. You can seed your own questions and answer them. "Do you serve Flemington?" "Do you offer free estimates?" Those are real questions your customers ask. Answer them in the profile before they have to call to find out.

**The description.** This isn't a sales pitch. It's a short, plain-English explanation of what you do and who you help. Write it like you're answering the question "what does this business do?" Keep it under 750 characters and skip the filler.

## One Thing to Check This Week

Search your own business name on Google. Look at the profile that comes up.

Ask yourself: if I'd never heard of this business and I saw this listing, would I call?

If the answer is uncertain, start with hours, category, and photos. Those three alone can change how the profile looks to someone deciding in under a minute.

Then look at your reviews. If you have customers who love what you do and haven't left a review, that's the easiest thing to fix. Send a follow-up message with a direct link to your review page. Don't ask for a five-star review. Ask them to share their experience.

## The Profile and the Website Work Together

A strong profile gets you found. A strong website gets you chosen. They're not competing, but the sequence matters.

For local service searches, the profile is often the first impression. If it's thin, your site doesn't get a chance. If it's strong, you're already ahead of the competitors who haven't touched theirs in two years.

The businesses that show up consistently in local searches tend to have both working. Complete profile, real photos, active reviews, and a site that's ready when someone clicks through to learn more.
