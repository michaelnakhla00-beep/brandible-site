You typed what you do and the name of your town into Google. Your competitors showed up. You didn't.

That's a real problem, and it's worth diagnosing correctly before you spend money trying to fix it.

There isn't one reason a business doesn't show up. There are usually a few working together. The good news is that most of them are fixable, and the fixes are not mysterious.

## You Might Not Be Claiming the Right Ground

Google shows local results based on relevance, distance, and popularity. [Local results are mainly based on relevance, distance, and popularity](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en). [Together, these factors help Google find the best match for customers' searches](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en). Your Google Business Profile is the foundation of how you show up in those local results on Maps and Search.

[Only verified businesses can show their business info on Maps and Search](https://support.google.com/business/answer/145585?hl=en). If you haven't claimed and verified yours, you're not playing the game.

[Businesses with complete and accurate info are more likely to show up in local search results. If your business info isn't accurate, your Business Profile might not show up for relevant searches in your area](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en). And if the profile you do have is incomplete or inaccurate, it may not appear for the searches that matter to you.

This is the first thing to check. Open Google, search your business name, and look at what shows up. If your profile is missing, unclaimed, or showing wrong information, that's your starting point.

## Your Profile Isn't Telling Google What You Do

Assuming your profile exists, the next question is whether it's doing its job.

[Relevance is how well a Business Profile matches what someone is searching for. To help Google better understand your business and match it to relevant searches, provide complete and detailed business info](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en). Google uses the information in your profile to match your business to searches. If the details are vague or missing, Google has less to work with.

[The categories you select affect your local ranking on Google](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7249669?hl=en). The categories you select affect your local ranking. [Your business categories inform your customers about what your business does](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7249669?hl=en). [It helps your business connect you with customers who search for your products or services](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7249669?hl=en). [Select a primary category that best describes your business. When you choose your category, select a specific category from the list that shows up. For example, instead of "Salon," choose "Nail salon."](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7249669?hl=en). So instead of selecting a broad category like "Contractor," you'd choose something like "Kitchen Remodeler" or "General Contractor" if that's the more specific, accurate fit.

[Do not exceed 750 characters in the description field](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3039617?hl=en). Your business description can be up to 750 characters. [Focus primarily on details about your business instead of details about promotions, prices, or sales](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3039617?hl=en). Use that space to explain what your business does and who you help. Don't fill it with promotions or pricing. [Business description , Enter a brief description of your business that shows in the "From the business" section of your Business Profile](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3039617?hl=en).

[You can update details like your address, hours, contact info, and photos to help customers find and learn more about your business](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3039617?hl=en). You can update your hours, contact info, address, and photos at any time. [Search results are based on algorithms that take a wide range of factors into account. When you edit your business info, it updates the search results, and changes can take up to 3 days to appear](https://support.google.com/business/answer/145585?hl=en). When you make changes, they can take up to three days to appear in search results.

## You Haven't Built Any Credibility Yet

Google's local ranking takes prominence into account. [Prominence means how well-known a business is. Prominent places are more likely to show up in search results. This factor's also based on info like how many websites link to your business and how many reviews you have. More reviews and positive ratings can help your business's local ranking](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en). That includes reviews, links to your site, and general signals that your business is real and trusted.

If you have no reviews, or a handful from two years ago, that's a gap. [When you reply to customer reviews, it shows that you value their feedback. Positive reviews and helpful replies can help your business stand out](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en).

This doesn't mean you need to chase reviews aggressively. It means you should make it easy for happy customers to leave one, and actually respond when they do.

## Your Website Might Be the Bottleneck

Your Google Business Profile and your website work together. If your site is slow, thin on content, or hard for Google to read, it can hold your local visibility back.

A few things worth checking:

- Does your site clearly state what you do and where you do it? A roofing company in Flemington whose website never mentions Flemington is leaving relevance on the table.
- Is your business name, address, and phone number consistent between your website, your Google profile, and anywhere else you're listed? Inconsistencies create confusion for Google and for customers.
- Does your site have any real pages for the services you offer, or is everything crammed into one scrolling page with no structure?

A site that [serves people clearly](/services/web-design/) tends to earn more trust from search engines too. That's not a coincidence.

## You Haven't Given It Enough Time

[Rankings for new businesses can take up to a month to appear in search results](https://support.google.com/business/answer/145585?hl=en). For new businesses, rankings can take up to a month to appear after verification. If you recently claimed or updated your profile, give it time before assuming something is broken.

## What to Fix First

If you're not showing up, work through this in order:

**1. Claim and verify your Google Business Profile** if you haven't already. [To show your business on Search, Maps, and other Google services, you must add or claim your business then verify your Business Profile](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3039617?hl=en).

**2. Fill in every field.** Categories, description, hours, phone number, website, address. [Keep your business info up-to-date: To improve your business visibility in local search results, make sure your business info is correct, complete, and up to date](https://support.google.com/business/answer/145585?hl=en). Complete and accurate information is the baseline.

**3. Add photos.** [You can show customers what you offer and tell the story of your business with photos and videos](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en). [To help make your Business Profile more attractive to customers, you can add photos or videos of your storefront, products, and services. When you add an exterior photo, it helps customers recognize your business when they visit](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3039617?hl=en). Real photos of your work, your space, or your team help your profile stand out and give customers something to trust.

**4. Get your first reviews, or respond to the ones you have.** You don't need 200. You need a handful of honest, recent ones, and replies that show you're paying attention.

**5. Look at your website.** If it doesn't name what you do and where you operate, fix that. If it's one page with no structure, that's worth addressing.

None of this is fast, but it's not complicated either. Local visibility is buildable. It just requires the right foundation.
